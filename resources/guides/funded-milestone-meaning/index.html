Freelance Payment Definition

A funded milestone means the money is committed, not yet earned.

Funding and releasing are separate events. Funding shows that the client has committed the agreed amount. Release occurs after the milestone reaches its settlement condition.

Funding is commitment

The money exists inside the payment workflow.

A promise to pay later is not a funded milestone. Neither is an edited screenshot, pending transfer or unrelated wallet transaction. Funding should be verified against the precise agreement.

Confirm the destination address.

Confirm the asset and delivered amount.

Check any required Destination Tag or memo.

Confirm the ledger transaction succeeded.

Funding is not release

The provider should not receive the money merely because it was deposited.

The funded amount remains connected to the milestone while work proceeds. Release follows delivery and the review rules; refund follows only the contract’s applicable conditions.

Funded: money is committed.

Active: work is underway.

Delivered: review can begin.

Released: payment has settled to the provider.

Why providers care

Verified funding reduces nonpayment uncertainty.

Before performing valuable work, a freelancer can see that the client completed the required funding action. This is stronger evidence than enthusiasm, reputation claims or a promise to pay after launch.

Do not start from an unfunded draft.

Match funding to the exact milestone.

Preserve the agreed amount and asset.

Record when the working clock begins.

Why clients care

Funding does not require surrendering every payment decision.

The client demonstrates ability and intent to pay while retaining the defined review process. Clear milestones limit the amount exposed to any one disagreement.

Review only against written scope.

Use included revisions appropriately.

Release accepted work promptly.

Cancel or refund remaining work only under stated rules.

Frequently asked questions

What Is a Funded Milestone?

What does funded milestone mean?

It means the agreed payment for a project stage has been committed but has not yet been released to the provider.

Is a funded milestone already paid to the freelancer?

No. Funding commits the amount; release settles it to the freelancer.

Should work begin before a milestone is funded?

Doing so exposes the provider to nonpayment risk and defeats a primary purpose of milestone funding.

Can a funded milestone be refunded?

It may be refundable under the agreement’s cancellation, expiry or failure conditions. Funding alone does not decide the outcome.
