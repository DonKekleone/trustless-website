Crypto Hiring Safety Guide

Vet a crypto freelancer before funding the first milestone.

A profile, follower count or confident direct message is not enough evidence for an irreversible payment. Good vetting connects the person, wallet, work history and proposed agreement before either side takes meaningful risk.

Check identity consistency

Look for a coherent trail rather than one impressive claim.

Compare the freelancer’s public profile, wallet address, portfolio, communication accounts and references. Mismatched names, copied work or pressure to move immediately deserve more investigation.

Confirm the same wallet across the agreement.

Inspect when public accounts were created.

Reverse-check suspicious portfolio samples.

Treat follower counts as weak evidence.

Verify work evidence

Ask for proof connected to the claimed role.

A developer can discuss architecture and show repositories or deployments. A designer can explain source files and decisions. A marketer can distinguish deliverables from outcomes instead of promising guaranteed reach.

Request relevant examples, not a giant generic portfolio.

Ask how the work was produced.

Check whether live examples still function.

Do not accept screenshots as the only evidence.

Test agreement quality

Serious providers can translate the request into scope.

The freelancer should identify deliverables, dependencies, deadlines, acceptance criteria and exclusions. Resistance to written milestones often predicts trouble later.

Ask what information they need from the client.

Require observable completion tests.

Discuss revisions and new scope.

Confirm the settlement asset and amount.

Start with limited exposure

A first contract should create evidence without risking the whole project.

Use a small meaningful milestone that produces independent value. Verify funding through the agreement, review the output and expand only after both parties demonstrate reliable behavior.

Avoid full irreversible prepayment.

Do not demand unpaid speculative work.

Use one reviewable first stage.

Increase commitment after successful settlement.

Frequently asked questions

How to Vet a Crypto Freelancer Before Hiring

How do I know whether a crypto freelancer is legitimate?

Check identity consistency, relevant work evidence, wallet details, communication quality and willingness to use a clear funded agreement.

Should I ask a freelancer for free sample work?

Usually no. A small paid milestone creates fairer evidence than substantial unpaid speculative work.

Can wallet history prove someone is trustworthy?

No. It can provide transaction evidence, but it does not prove identity, competence or future behavior.

What is the safest first project?

Use a small independently valuable milestone with explicit scope, verified funding and a finite review process.
