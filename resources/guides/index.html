Before money moves

Start with the agreement—not the transaction.

On-chain payments are final. A clean contract begins with clear scope, correct wallet addresses and terms both parties understand before funding.

  1. Use a compatible XRPL wallet

    Connect through the supported wallet flow. Your public XRPL address identifies you; never share a seed phrase or private key.

  2. Build your Network identity

    Add a display name, title, biography and profile image so counterparties can understand who they are dealing with.

  3. Define the work precisely

    Record the deliverable, XRP amount, delivery window, review period, included revisions and milestone structure.

  4. Verify the counterparty

    Confirm the public profile and XRPL address independently. A familiar name or profile image is not proof of wallet ownership.

  5. Read the funding instructions

    Amount, destination address, Destination Tag and Memo may all matter. Copy them from the active contract rather than from chat.

Contract lifecycle

The rules keep moving when people go quiet.

Each agreement progresses through explicit states. Review the live contract for its exact deadlines because the enforcement clock—not a private message—controls the next action.

  1. Draft or created

    Terms are assembled and can be reviewed before any XRP is sent.

  2. Awaiting funding

    The system provides the required payment details. The contract is not active merely because someone claims to have paid.

  3. Funded and active

    Payment verification starts the active agreement and its enforcement timeline from the recorded ledger event.

  4. Work phase

    The provider completes the current deliverable within the agreed delivery window and records delivery in the contract workflow.

  5. Review or revision

    The client reviews the submitted work. If revision rights remain, the contract can enter a defined revision window.

  6. Release, refund or cancellation

    The contract resolves according to the recorded actions, milestone state and enforcement rules.

  7. Completed and rated

    The resolved agreement becomes part of the parties’ visible operating history.
Rule Typical behavior What the user should do
Delivery window Set when the agreement or milestone is created. Deliver and record completion before the enforcement clock expires.
Review window The buyer receives a defined period to review delivered work. Approve, request an allowed revision or take the available contract action.
Revision window Applies when revisions are included and properly requested. Keep the request specific and return the revision within the displayed deadline.
Extension An eligible milestone can support one recorded extension request. Request or decide the extension inside the workroom—not through an informal promise.
Silence Automation may advance a contract when a deadline passes without action. Watch the enforcement clock. Silence is not a pause button.

Contract configuration can vary. Always follow the deadlines and controls displayed inside the specific funded agreement.

XRPL payment safety

Verify every field before signing.

A valid transaction sent with incorrect identifying information may not fund the intended contract. Treat the funding panel as an exact instruction set.

Confirm the destination

Compare the complete XRPL destination address. Do not rely on the first or last few characters alone.

Use the exact XRP amount

Send the amount requested by the contract. Do not round it or subtract network fees from it.

Include the Destination Tag

If a tag is supplied, include it exactly. It can identify which contract should receive credit for the deposit.

Preserve the Memo

If the workflow supplies a Memo, keep it attached exactly as shown. Tags and memos are not interchangeable.

Wait for verification

A wallet confirmation proves submission, not necessarily contract recognition. Wait until the contract itself reports funding.

Keep the transaction hash

The XRPL transaction hash is the durable receipt. Save it before closing the funding flow.

Trustless Enforcer

Objective conditions. Automated outcome.

The Enforcer is designed for agreements where settlement depends on a measurable condition rather than subjective approval of delivered work.

  1. Define the payment

    Enter the net XRP amount and verify the intended recipient address.

  2. Identify the asset correctly

    Confirm the token issuer or identifying information used by the monitoring system.

  3. Set the target and expiry

    Choose an objective target and a deadline that both parties understand.

  4. Fund exactly as instructed

    Use the supplied address, amount, tag and other identifying payment data.

  5. Let the rule resolve

    If the condition is satisfied before expiry, the defined release path applies. If not, the defined refund path applies.
The Enforcer executes the condition selected by users. It does not promise market performance, verify promotional effort or turn an unrealistic target into a fair agreement.

Security rules

Automation removes discretion—not responsibility.

Trustless reduces dependence on promises, but users still control the quality of the contract, the accuracy of payment inputs and the wallets they authorize.

Never share wallet secrets

No legitimate guide, profile, support message or counterparty needs your seed phrase or private key.

Do not fund from chat instructions

Return to the official contract and compare every payment field before signing.

Read before approving

Release, cancellation and refund actions can have permanent economic consequences.

Respect the timer

A deadline continues running unless the contract records a valid extension or state change.

Use milestones for larger work

Smaller deliverables reduce ambiguity and prevent one disagreement from controlling the entire agreement.

Verify official domains

Use trustlesspayments.io for corporate documentation and trustlessnetwork.app for the Network application.

Troubleshooting

When something does not look right.

Problem First checks
Payment is not verified Compare the destination, exact amount, Destination Tag, Memo and transaction status on XRPL.
Contract still says awaiting funding Confirm the payment was sent to the current contract instructions and allow the verification process to complete.
Wrong wallet appears connected Disconnect, confirm the intended account inside the wallet and reconnect through the official application.
Action is unavailable Check your participant role, current milestone, contract phase and whether the action window remains open.
Extension cannot be requested Check whether the milestone is eligible or its available extension has already been used.
Contract moved to Resolved Inspect the recorded terminal status: completed, refunded, cancelled or expired.

Quick answers

Trustless user guide FAQ.

Does Trustless hold my wallet keys?

Never provide a seed phrase or private key. Users authorize supported transactions through their own wallet flow.

Is creating a draft the same as funding a contract?

No. A draft records proposed terms. The agreement becomes funded only after the required XRP payment is recognized by the contract system.

Why are Destination Tags and Memos important?

They can associate a shared destination payment with the correct contract. Omitting or changing them can prevent automatic matching.

Can a private message change the contract rules?

Do not assume so. Important actions should be reflected inside the contract workflow and its recorded state.

What happens when a party stays silent?

The result depends on the contract phase and displayed enforcement rules. Deadlines can advance the workflow even when a participant does nothing.

Should a large project use one payment?

Milestones are usually clearer for larger work because delivery, review and settlement happen in smaller defined stages.

Is Trustless affiliated with Ripple?

No. Trustless Payments is not affiliated with Ripple. XRP Ledger is open-source decentralized blockchain technology.
