Crypto Payment Fee Guide

Who pays crypto transaction fees? The agreement should answer before settlement.

A crypto payment can include network fees, platform charges and later conversion costs. If the contract only states a headline amount, the parties may disagree about whether the provider must receive that amount net or whether fees reduce it.

Separate the fee types

Different costs arise from different actions.

Network fees pay for blockchain transactions. Platform fees pay for a service or enforcement workflow. Exchange spreads and withdrawal fees arise when assets are converted or moved elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

Who Pays Crypto Transaction Fees?

Does the sender or receiver pay crypto network fees?

The sender often pays the fee for initiating a transaction, but the agreement should state the intended allocation.

Can transaction fees reduce a freelancer’s payment?

They can if the contract defines a gross amount with deductions; net-payment terms require the provider to receive the stated amount.

Are platform fees the same as blockchain fees?

No. Platform fees pay for a service, while network fees pay for ledger transactions.

Who pays conversion fees after receiving crypto?

Usually the party choosing to convert or withdraw pays those optional costs unless the agreement says otherwise.
