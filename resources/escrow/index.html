What Escrow Does

Escrow creates a defined path between funding and settlement.

The purpose of escrow is not to make every transaction risk-free. It is to replace part of the uncertainty with agreed conditions. The parties define what is being funded, what must happen next and which outcomes can release or return funds.

Before funding

Confirm the amount, recipient, deliverables, deadlines, review periods and settlement rules before money moves.

After funding

The agreement moves through defined states instead of depending entirely on messages, screenshots or informal promises.

Release

Funds can move toward the recipient when the applicable release conditions have been satisfied.

Refund

A refund path should be understood before funding so both sides know which conditions can return unsettled funds.

Milestone Escrow

Large agreements do not need to become one large risk.

Milestones divide work and payment into smaller stages. Instead of exposing the entire agreement at once, each stage can have its own deliverable, review period and settlement decision.

Smaller exposure

Breaking work into stages can reduce the amount of unfinished work and unsettled value tied to a single decision.

Clear checkpoints

Each milestone creates a place to review progress before the next stage of the agreement continues.

Before You Fund

Good escrow starts with a clear agreement.

Escrow cannot repair vague terms after the fact. Read the payment instructions, verify the destination details and understand the release, refund and review rules before sending funds.
